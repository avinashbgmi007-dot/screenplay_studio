# Writer-first feedback experience — working plan

**Status:** proposal for discussion, not an approved design or implementation. No application code changed. The generated concept images explore moments in the journey; they are not a consistent or validated prototype. For the consolidated recommendations, dependencies, priorities and outstanding critique gate, see [OVERALL_PRODUCT_PLAN.md](OVERALL_PRODUCT_PLAN.md).

## Product promise
A writer can understand a reading, check what supports it, disagree without penalty, explore ideas and retain control of the draft. A consultant can inspect scope, counter-evidence and provenance rather than receiving a polished verdict with no audit trail.

## One desk, one journey

1. **Analysis complete.** The existing writing desk remains in place. A dismissible, reopenable first reading briefly describes an evidenced strength and an important question. Offer immediate access to the full reading, and say if analysis is incomplete or demo-generated. Do not fabricate a strength if none is supported.
2. **Full reading.** A compact, navigable index covers every delivered finding, strengths, scope and analysis limitations. The writer can group by story/scene, filter or search; filters and counts must remain honest. A note can be opened next to its relevant scene without losing the index.
3. **A note.** Present observation, source passage(s), interpretation, potential impact, counter-reading and relevant craft lens as applicable. Distinguish exact text, approximate match, scene reference, and summary-derived judgment. “Passage found” is never proof of the interpretation. For global observations link several scenes and disclose coverage gaps; do not demand a quote where the claim cannot honestly be supported by one.
4. **Discuss or disagree.** Preserve selected note, cited scene text, draft version, limitations and the writer's intent when moving to Sushruta or Sameer. Sushruta investigates the diagnosis, can retract it or maintain an evidenced disagreement. Sameer first discusses and challenges the writer's thinking; he explores approaches or suggests changes when explicitly asked, and can agree to leave the scene alone. Neither should invent evidence or claim material they did not receive.
5. **Explore an edit.** Proposals are separate from the current draft; compare with original, then explicitly apply or discard. If the target changed since the proposal was created, refuse a blind apply and offer refresh/compare. Preserve undo and screenplay formatting.
6. **Re-analysis.** Preserve writer decisions and prior notes. Distinguish passage changed, re-raised, not raised, uncertain correspondence, and incomplete analysis. A changed passage or missing note is not proof that the issue is resolved. Ask the writer before carrying a decision across an ambiguous match.

A single contextual companion area can host review and conversations; it must not become a permanently crowded dashboard. The writer can close it and keep writing. The precise layout and defaults remain design hypotheses.

## Findings quality architecture

- **Parsing first:** make missing pages, OCR and scene-boundary uncertainty visible. Test PDF, FDX and text/Fountain, including multilingual and code-switched scripts.
- **Candidate vs judgment:** identify observations first, then assess them against full relevant passages, wider story context, writer intent where supplied, and plausible counter-evidence. An additional model review is a fallible check, not an independent proof of correctness.
- **Finding record:** versioned draft and analysis, scope, observed facts and source locations, match type, interpretation, rationale, counter-considerations, knowledge-base rule and attribution, limitations, disposition and provenance. Preserve raw candidates and reasons for merges/withdrawals.
- **Delivery:** confirmed mechanical observations, grounded interpretive notes and insufficiently supported questions have distinct wording. Prioritize by demonstrated story impact and relevance, not rule-default severity alone. Never silently hide failed coverage or suppress legitimate dissenting views.
- **Knowledge base:** audit rule signals, required inputs, exceptions, attribution, genre routing and overlapping or prescriptive rules. Use principles as lenses, not compulsory formulae. A rule's confidence tier is not the model's probability of being right.
- **Continuity:** retain the existing ledger and separate writer truth verdict from disposition. Reconcile across runs with evidence and a reviewable uncertain state; do not assume changing a hash solves model variance.

## Collaborators

| | Sushruta — consultant | Sameer — co-writer |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Independently evaluate and discuss a reading | Help the writer think, question and create without taking over |
| Required | Specific evidence, limitations, openness to correction or reasoned disagreement | Listen, critique and ask useful questions; offer suggestions when explicitly requested |
| Avoid | Verdict-first certainty, forced agreement, forced wit, removing meaningful uncertainty | Automatic agreement, forced disagreement, unsolicited solutions or rewrites, excessive questioning |
| Shared | Transparent context limitations, no invented quotations, writer approval before edits | Same |

Audit post-generation transforms for changed meaning, and streaming for raw text that is later corrected. Measure context selection so the selected note and evidence outrank unrelated history or persona flourishes.

## Execution and decision gates

**0 — Baseline:** inventory real workflows and model/hardware configurations; run the existing tests and representative live analyses. Build a consented, diverse evaluation set: short and feature scripts; multilingual and code-switching; genre and experimental forms; FDX, Fountain and PDF including OCR. Record parsing coverage, actual evidence depth, error cases, latency, model and settings. Independent writers and consultants label correctness, partial correctness, usefulness, harm and important missed issues. Report denominators, reviewer disagreement and uncertainty; avoid treating self-selected user verdicts as population accuracy.

**1 — Interaction prototype:** make one consistent clickable prototype using Screenplay Studio terms and sample data. Test first-reading, full review, multi-scene evidence, disagreement, proposal and re-analysis; include keyboard, small screens, incomplete analysis and empty states. No product implementation until the flow is approved.

**2 — Finding contract and KB audit:** propose a versioned schema, provenance and migration of existing reports, marks and sessions. Review KB examples and exceptions with domain experts. Design against the baseline and test both harmful false positives and lost true notes. Never silently overwrite writer state.

**3 — Controlled vertical slice:** after approval, implement one end-to-end finding through parsing, validation, delivery, conversation and optional revision, retaining existing paths until migration is verified. Add regression and failure-injection tests.

**4 — Evaluate and scale:** compare with baseline across model/hardware configurations. Assess expert-rated precision, important-issue recall, writer usefulness and agency, persona consistency, completeness and p50/p95 latency. Set explicit thresholds with stakeholders after baseline; if a dimension worsens, investigate before rollout. Stage release, preserve rollback and monitor with opt-in privacy-respecting feedback.

## Performance and privacy

Keep local-first processing. Benchmark rather than presume concurrency helps a single local model. Prioritize selected-scene/context retrieval, bounded generation, caching keyed by draft/model/prompt/KB version, and incremental re-analysis only where dependencies are known. Disclose stale and partial results. Measure time to first usable output and end-to-end completion separately; never show unvalidated content as final merely to appear fast.

## Self-critique and unresolved decisions

- A companion panel may still feel distracting; user testing must compare it with a review-first arrangement.
- Requiring stronger evidence risks losing valuable subjective observations; benchmark recall as well as precision.
- Multi-pass checks can increase local-model latency and repeat the same model's bias. Evaluate benefit per cost.
- Intent is not always known; ask only when useful and do not turn every finding into a questionnaire.
- This plan is based on targeted implementation inspection, not a verified review of every repository file. No live-model quality run or writer study was performed in this session; pytest was unavailable in the environment. Existing historical measurements are not current validation.

## Additional source audit — priorities before design sign-off

1. **P0: lost story context.** `screenplay_analyzer/pipeline.py` truncates each model-facing scene to 2,200 characters and the ordered scene overview to 6,000 characters. This can cut off later scenes or the end of a scene; a truncation marker alone does not establish what was actually reviewed. Audit each pass and report included/omitted ranges. Evaluate end-of-story recall on feature scripts before selecting a replacement (e.g. bounded hierarchical retrieval that explicitly preserves coverage). The observation pass also caps supplied script text at 24,000 characters.
2. **P0: accuracy label.** `screenplay_studio/revision.py:verdict_accuracy` uses (correct + partial) / judged as its headline and has a 98.58 target. That is a user-agreement metric among voluntarily judged notes, not validated accuracy; partial findings can still be harmful. Show separate counts for correct, partial, incorrect and unjudged, with coverage. Assess quality separately through independent blinded review and missed-issue analysis.
3. **P0: misleading completion.** `finding_statuses` uses a changed or removed quote as an automatic `addressed` signal; proposal: call it “passage changed — review” and reserve resolved for an explicit writer decision. Preserve existing stored data and reconcile its historical meaning in migration.
4. **P1: KB calibration.** The 26 rule files currently contain 263 rules; 202 are labelled high confidence. These are author-assigned tiers, not measured predictive accuracy. Review the most influential high-confidence rules and actual pass inputs with consultants; never translate the tier into certainty about a particular finding.
5. **P1: cost versus benefit.** The default observation pass can attempt 24 model calls and verifies a citation's presence, not its support for the diagnosis. Its source documents historical measured results on two scripts, not a current benchmark. Compare selective, batched and on-demand approaches against the existing pass on the same models and scripts, including failure and recall; preserve provenance. No promised latency or quality gain without measurement.
6. **P1: meaning-preserving persona and streaming.** The chat engine streams generated tokens before final quote and persona guards; persona-specific post-processing can remove meaningful hedges. Test raw-versus-final divergence and uncertainty preservation, including single-quoted/short passages outside the quote guard's current checks. Decide between buffered verification and explicitly provisional streaming using measured quality and latency.
7. **P1: context and language.** Chat uses a character-based context approximation and can shed craft principles/rationale; it caps scene retrieval per turn. Use model tokenization where available, reserve space for the selected note and evidence, and surface omissions to writers. Independently assess language-filter false positives and language understanding with native readers instead of penalizing code-switching.

Audit note: the integrity gate's own withdrawal ledger preserves full items entering that gate. Other earlier filtering and deduplication have different accounting paths (for example, language-filter stats retain issue excerpts). A proposed end-to-end audit trail therefore needs coverage across **all** removal and merge stages, not just the integrity gate.

## Reproducibility check — synthetic, no model

A temporary Python environment outside the repository was created with runtime dependencies and pytest. Eight selected analyzer, KB, persona, verification and consultation test modules passed: **166 passed**. This supersedes the earlier note that pytest was unavailable; it is not a full-suite or live-model result.

Direct synthetic probes against the current functions confirm:
- With 60 fabricated scenes and lengthy summaries, the 6,000-character overview contains scene 1 but not scene 60 and includes a truncation marker. A 2,300+ character scene loses its ending and includes a marker. This establishes the mechanism, not its frequency on real scripts.
- For a whole-script observation request on the same artificial document, `_script_text` returned the first 24,000 characters without the ending of scene 60 and without a truncation marker in that returned text. This does not establish what other UI disclosure might exist.
- The Sushruta persona transform turns “Maybe the scene is deliberately quiet.” into “the scene is deliberately quiet.”, removing the uncertainty. This is a deterministic example, not a measured frequency in live replies.

These checks strengthen the priority of coverage-aware context selection, meaning-preserving persona behavior and empirical evaluation; they do not establish the rate of false findings or quality of any model.

### Real-page verification probe — controlled, fabricated findings

Using the parsed final scene, a deliberate alteration of a real line (replacing “cut” with “call”) was still marked `verified` by the quote verifier with fuzzy similarity 0.84. This is expected from the lenient threshold, but a user-facing exact-quote label would be misleading. The quoted phrase's normalized original verifies at 1.0. This result tests matching, not semantic entailment or a model's likelihood of producing the alteration.

A separate fabricated claim “Ram never speaks in this scene,” paired with a real quote from that scene, receives `verified` for the quote and `True` from `verify_observation` even though the parsed scene contains nine Ram dialogue elements. Crucially, the existing `FindingIntegrityGate`, when run on this candidate and the parsed document, rejects it and records the withdrawal as an absence claim falsified by presence. The probe therefore demonstrates both an important individual-check limitation and a working downstream safeguard; it does NOT show that this fabricated note would be delivered by the full analyzer or establish a false-positive frequency.

Recommendation: exact and approximate citation labels must differ; semantic support and counter-evidence require separate assessment. Retain and test the existing absence safeguard, including paraphrases and multilingual variants, rather than replacing it on the basis of a synthetic example.

### Propagation and screenplay-specific evaluation plan

The real parse places the two flashback markers in `characters_present`; the knowledge graph consequently creates two character entries for them. Their dialogue counts are empty. This proves graph contamination, not that a delivered report or chat response necessarily mentions them. Internal scene 5 also includes the post-flashback montage before the next explicit scene heading; whether to split that montage is an editorial/parser decision needing source-aware review, not an assumed error.

A consent-based evaluation of this particular script, once a real local model is available:

1. **Establish source truth.** A Telugu/Tenglish-capable reader checks PDF pages against parsed headings, source page ranges, scene IDs, character cues, dialogue attribution and the final sequence. Label ambiguous boundaries rather than forcing an answer. Compare with FDX/Fountain if the writer can supply it.
2. **Freeze a baseline.** Record draft hash, parser version, KB version, model/quantization, context settings, hardware, analysis settings and random seed where supported. Run multiple times on the *same unchanged draft*; retain candidate, delivered and withdrawn findings plus actual context ranges and latency. Never treat the demo model as a quality baseline.
3. **Judge blind.** Independent writer/consultant reviewers assess each candidate's factual basis, citation fidelity, relevance, impact, alternatives and potential harm; use correct/partial/incorrect/insufficient-context separately. Reviewers also independently name important issues the report missed. Resolve disagreements by discussion; report agreement and uncertain cases. Treat the writer's intent as a separate input, not an automatic ground-truth verdict about every audience response.
4. **Target this script's risks.** Check the flashback boundaries and bogus character entries; ensure Ajay/Darshi/Ram moments and the final scene are in scope for arc or ending claims; verify printed-vs-internal scene references; inspect fuzzy quotations and claims of absence; test Tenglish and cultural nuance with a qualified reader. Do not assume silence or unusual staging is a defect.
5. **Test personas with the same evidence.** Ask Sushruta to explain and then reconsider one challenged note; ask Sameer to preserve the writer's intention and explore options; test a missing-context question and a request not to rewrite. Evaluate grounding, correction, agency, distinct voice, language handling and response latency. Freeze prompts/configuration; compare raw streamed and final output.
6. **Test the journey.** Let the writer review the first reading, find the original scene, disagree, defer, compare a proposal without applying, then rerun the same draft and a changed draft. Observe time, comprehension and errors, not just preference. Verify that a vanished finding is not announced as fixed and that ambiguous marks are reviewable.

A single screenplay is an excellent case study but cannot establish general accuracy, fairness, or model performance. Broaden to diverse, consented scripts before release. The writer controls external sharing, and results should be stored locally by default.

### Writer-intent handoff — implementation review

`app.js:discussFinding` seeds “how should I approach fixing it?” and `discussWithDoctor` seeds “What exactly is wrong?” Both frame a finding as a defect before the writer has judged it. Each pins `evidence_quote || issue`; `CoWriterEngine.send_message` labels a pinned payload as a passage from the script. When no evidence quote exists, this path risks representing the analyst's issue text as if it were the writer's words. The report summary can separately be included in context, and chat history may preserve intent; this inspection does not prove the intent is always lost. However, the inspected handoff does not explicitly package the complete selected finding, evidence provenance, multiple scene references and writer-stated intent together.

Proposed design: typed context items (`script_excerpt`, `finding`, `writer_intent`) with source and draft version; a visible editable intent scoped to a finding or project, never silently treated as universal or permanent. Keep “discuss” neutral; make “explore a fix” an explicit choice. The writer can edit/delete intent, and both personas must be told when relevant context is missing. Add tests for no-quote findings, multi-scene notes, switching partners, dissent, stale drafts, context-budget trimming and false attribution. Confirm actual session and persistence behavior end-to-end before making a migration plan.

### Dialogue-aware coverage experiment — Pain 3

Using the same disposable cue-and-dialogue grouping simulation and approximate 1,600-character / 250-character overlap target: 22 parsed scenes yielded 33 windows covering all 31,327 nonempty element-text characters; 33,755 element-text characters were included with repetition (7.8% overhead). ## Benchmark readiness and multilingual evaluation risk

This does not rule out models outside checked paths or other services, but there is no usable real model established here. Do not use the canned demo model as a quality benchmark or imply a meaningful model comparison was run.

The existing `tests/eval_recall.py` is explicitly a lexical, non-gating aid; its `_norm` strips everything except ASCII letters and digits. This is a limitation of the **evaluation helper**, not a claim about production quote matching or both attached scripts (which are predominantly romanized). Even an improved lexical match is not a substitute for blind semantic adjudication. Record exact denominators and abstentions and do not treat 100% lexical matches as creative recall.

A current-pipeline baseline can be run only where a real compatible local model is reachable: parse a consented PDF with `python -m screenplay_parser parse <file> -o <private-path>/parsed.json`, then run `python -m screenplay_analyzer <private-path>/parsed.json --server http://localhost:8080 -o <private-path>/baseline.md`. ## Collaborator behavior audit — writer-led default

The current Sameer persona contains useful instructions to ask before unrequested opinions and to stay grounded, but also instructs “one concrete way forward”; brainstorm mode explicitly generates directions. `peer.should_probe` recognizes only a narrow English syntax and, in a direct probe, classifies “Give me three options for the ending” and “Please suggest two possibilities” as unreasoned ideas, so it asks for a probe despite the explicit request. `cap_suggestions` then truncates a bulleted multi-option answer to one even if the writer explicitly asked for three. These are deterministic tests of helper behavior, not live model performance. The engine's `should_probe` branch is not gated on persona and appends a prompt identifying the assistant as Sameer even if a different persona is active: test real effect rather than presume all doctor turns drift.

Design contract: no unsolicited solutions by default; thoughtful critique, observations or questions are welcome; answer direct questions; respond to explicit requests for options and preserve requested breadth; ask when permission is ambiguous rather than relying solely on English regexes. Respect a writer's current turn and an editable preference, with the current request taking precedence. Avoid automatic follow-up questions, over-rigidity or silent truncation. Test English/Telugu/Tenglish, direct requests, emotional statements, 'just listen', one-vs-many requested ideas, persona isolation, refusal of a rewrite, and conversation continuity. Sushruta provides his own evidence-based judgment and may maintain a reasoned disagreement after hearing the writer's intent; intent is context, not a veto on critique. No product change or real-model validation performed.

## Human-feeling collaborators who evolve with the writer

Writer requirement: Sameer and Sushruta should feel like distinct, naturally evolving people at the desk, not robotic workflows. Their personalities can be warm, imperfect in expression, playful or serious in different ways, and capable of reasoned disagreement. They remain clearly identified as AI collaborators; do not claim real professional credentials, real-world memories or experiences they do not have.

- **Sameer:** a curious creative peer, writer-led by default. He listens, remembers context, challenges ideas, shares an honest reaction when invited, and does not offer fixes unasked or take over. When requested, he responds to the requested degree of help. He can have natural humor and distinct taste without formulaic praise or mandated questions.
- **Sushruta:** an independent, thoughtful consultant, direct but humane. He brings an evidence-based point of view, listens to the writer's intention, may revise his view or disagree with reasons, and never performs certainty or cruelty. He can appreciate strengths without fake praise.
- **Evolution:** use explicit, inspectable, editable, deletable writer preferences and project-scoped creative intent, plus bounded conversation context. Preferences (e.g. discuss before suggesting) are not facts about a script; writer intent is not proof of audience impact. Ask before saving sensitive or personal information, never silently infer unchangeable traits, and avoid cross-project leakage. Offer clear scope and deletion controls; the writer can override any preference in a turn.
- **Quality:** maintain personality without sacrificing quote fidelity, uncertainty, language and cultural nuance, or latency. Prefer response guidance and selective examples over destructive word-stripping. Evaluate realistic multi-turn exchanges for memory correctness, persona distinctness, appropriate initiative, naturalness, evidence and correction, Tenglish/Telugu, and false familiarity. Measure real-model performance; do not assume prompt changes guarantee behavior.

### Relationship-memory audit — writer-led preference

The existing implementation already has writer-level relationship dimensions, project-scoped observations, a visible “Sameer's notes on you” surface and suppression endpoints. A global writer profile is loaded for sessions, and past-project library digests are deliberately used as context with a separation guard. Clearing chat intentionally retains relationship memory. These are existing capabilities; do not assume that suppression means secure deletion or that a full edit/delete/consent workflow exists without auditing it.

Direct helper probe: `extract_signals("Don't give me ideas unless I ask.", "idea", False)` emits `support_style: generate`, the opposite of the explicit preference, because the regex matches “give me” without interpreting negation. One such signal alone does not trigger a preference gate: the system requires repeated evidence and other signals can change it. This demonstrates a misclassified signal, not proof of a changed profile or real reply. Proposed fix path: explicitly saved, editable writer instructions take priority over weak inferred patterns and one-turn requests override global defaults; handle negation, context, Tenglish/Telugu, scope and consent; make provenance, retention, suppress vs delete and cross-project use visible. Validate suppression and deletion end-to-end. Test both collaborators separately; a preference for Sameer not to offer ideas unasked should not prevent Sushruta's independent critique.

### Memory controls and scope — additional probes

The UI's “forget this” calls `WriterMemory.suppress`: in a temporary profile the observation remained in the JSON file with `suppressed: true`. It is hidden from the notes view and excluded from the relationship card under the current gate, but this is suppression, not deletion; it can be re-learned. Clearing a chat session deletes conversation history but deliberately keeps the relationship profile. Do not claim secure erasure; a future explicit delete must consider copies, backups, concurrent refresh and already-derived memories.

The refresh observation-scope helper uses presence of known character entities to assign project scope. In a direct helper probe, “The ending deliberately leaves their relationship unresolved.” was assigned `global`, while an observation naming Ajay was assigned `project:sample`. This illustrates mis-scoping risk; it is not proof that this exact observation was generated, delivered across projects or appeared in a card. Prompt and confidence gates also affect what is used. Proposed solution: explicit provenance and scope at creation rather than name detection as the primary boundary, private by default, writer review/correction, and tests for generic plot facts without names, overlapping character names, cross-project digest and deletion races. Make clear what the two personas and shelf digest use, while allowing independent judgment and consent-based cross-project recall.

### Memory lifecycle and concurrent refresh review

- UI “forget this” suppresses an item, not deletion. The implementation allows a belief to be re-learned; clearing a session retains the writer profile. Deleting a project removes its directory but the shown route does not remove observations stored in the writer-wide profile. Scope of external backups and all other derived data is not yet fully audited.
- `memory.py` keeps refresh scope and entity names in module-global state, set before a model call; refresh merges after the call and replay uses the same globals. A deterministic simulated interleaving (A sets A context, B sets B context, then A merges) assigned a fabricated A observation to global. This demonstrates the shared-state hazard; no concurrent production incident or cross-project exposure was observed. Per-call immutable scope must travel with refresh proposals and replay; a stale refresh must not restore a deleted or edited memory.
- The doctor's cross-project case file calls quote-change-derived statuses “findings addressed via edits” and computes followthrough. Since this status is not proof of a resolved concern, the doctor could receive misleading historical context. Replace with neutral wording and writer decisions, and assess whether cross-project summaries are wanted; do not treat past model notes as objective judgments about the writer.

## Proposed memory contract — design decision, not implementation

### What each collaborator may remember

| Information | Default scope | Origin and authority | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| General collaboration preference | Writer-wide, separately configurable per collaborator | Explicit writer preference outranks an inference; current request outranks stored preference | Shape initiative, tone and level of help, not force agreement |
| Creative intention or story fact | Current project and relevant draft/revision | Writer-stated intention or source-linked fact, labelled distinctly | Inform discussion; neither is proof of audience impact |
| A consultant's interpretation | Current project and draft, attributed to the consultant | Revisable judgment, never writer intent or objective fact | Continue an independent discussion, including disagreement |
| Inferred preference | Initially a private suggestion for writer review | Evidence-linked and fallible; not a fixed personality judgment | Only applied according to the writer's memory settings |
| Other project or shelf context | Not shared across projects by default | Explicit, reversible writer choice | Only when relevant; never confuse with the active draft |

The writer can request either collaborator's own past position, but neither should claim an invented personal history. Keep the two personas distinct; avoid turning the memory interface into a questionnaire.

### Pause, edit, delete

- **Pause:** prevent retrieval and new learning for the chosen scope and collaborators until resumed; show that the records still exist. One-turn context is still usable in the active conversation unless the writer says otherwise; explain the difference.
- **Edit:** change content, scope and collaborator visibility with provenance and a revision/version. Treat edits as the writer's statement, and do not silently replace them with a model inference. Current turn can still request a different style temporarily.
- **Delete:** remove targeted active records and dependent derived entries, prevent an in-flight refresh from restoring them, invalidate caches, and disclose separately whether chat history, exports and backups remain. Suppression is not deletion. A writer can explicitly choose to re-add information; automatic re-inference from old retained chats is disabled for deleted material unless the writer opts back in.
- **Delete project:** present explicit choices for its chats, project memories and cross-project derivatives; do not imply the current route already performs all of these. Keep unrelated projects and explicit writer-wide preferences intact.

### Concurrency and recovery contract

1. Carry project ID, persona, source version and scope as immutable values with each refresh proposal—never module-global mutable state. Distinguish writer-wide and project records without guessing based on character names.
2. At commit, use the existing cross-process store lock and compare the relevant memory/privacy generation with the one captured before the model call. A pause, edit, delete or project removal invalidates incompatible stale proposals; do not hold a file lock across model inference.
3. A minimal deletion/suppression tombstone or equivalent version guard prevents replay and automatic re-creation from existing old chats; it must not retain sensitive content unnecessarily. Explicit re-add by the writer is a separate action. Define retention and access of tombstones and backups before promising deletion.
4. Remove/invalidate relevant in-memory caches and derived summaries; report partial failures instead of claiming deletion succeeded. Review lock ordering and migration compatibility with the existing atomic JSON stores.

### Acceptance tests before shipping

- Two simultaneous project refreshes using overlapping and absent character names never cross-scope—even after save/replay and process restart.
- Pause, edit, suppress and delete during a waiting model call cannot be undone by the stale result; perform actual two-thread and cross-process tests. A new, explicit writer request can re-add a preference.
- Both personas receive only the scopes they are allowed; the active turn overrides a remembered preference. Writer-specific Sameer preferences do not silence Sushruta's evidence-based disagreement.
- “Forget” vs deletion is explained accurately; verify active stores, derived entries, cached digests and restart behavior. Clearly disclose retained chat, exports and backup limitations. No forensic-erasure guarantee.
- Test human outcomes with writers over several sessions: appropriate recollection, no false familiarity, conversational naturalness, independent critique and requested level of initiative. Track misremembering and privacy errors separately from whether a personality feels pleasant.

Self-critique: pause semantics, cross-project default and the desired effort of memory review need writer testing. Tombstones can themselves retain sensitive information; version guards and data minimization must be reviewed. A genuine evolution of style needs long-term consented study, not merely a larger profile.

### Language filtering: lifecycle and decision

The analysis pipeline's language filter records only count and first 200 characters of each removed issue in `stats.language_filtered`; it does not preserve the complete removed finding there. The separate `withdrawals` ledger records integrity-gate removals, not these language-filter removals. `_sanitize_report` additionally applies the filter when serving old reports, without adding a corresponding language-removal record in the inspected path. So “reviewable withdrawal audit” above is a proposed capability, not an existing one; a truncated issue alone cannot recover the evidence and reasoning of a filtered candidate. A fresh analysis computes evidence counts after its own filtering; the previously reproduced stale-count mismatch is specifically at serve-time sanitization of an already stored report, not all fresh reports.

Product decision: recommend a locally computed, conservative three-way disposition: (a) clear meta-commentary can be kept out of the primary reading; (b) clear story/craft notes proceed through normal evidence validation; (c) ambiguous cases remain reviewable, without automatically presenting them as findings. Record the complete candidate, reason, relevant context and filter version in a project-local audit store with the same privacy/deletion rules as the report. Avoid an additional model call on every finding; optionally review only ambiguous cases on request or when justified by benchmarked benefit. Surface language capability failure as a concise coverage limitation without treating the writing's language as a defect.

Trade-offs: broad filtering is cheap but may suppress useful creative observations; show-all is more transparent but overwhelms the writer; mandatory model rechecks add latency and can introduce another layer of confident mistakes. The proposed local-first triage trades some engineering complexity and local storage for recoverability, while reserving expensive review for cases where it demonstrably helps. No deterministic method can reliably infer intent from keywords alone; the decision boundaries require multilingual consultant and writer adjudication, including deliberate subtitles, code-switching, actual comprehension failure and feedback which mentions language incidentally. Audit records must not become a hidden permanent copy after project deletion, or silently reappear in normal feedback.

### Contradiction guard: false-positive probe and decision

A controlled direct call to `apply_evidence_floor` with fabricated observations showed that “He asks her to stay; however, she leaves” was marked `contested` and high severity was demoted to medium, even though that observation describes the same event as the synthetic issue. “The scene supplies no evidence of his stated motive” was likewise marked contested solely by the words “no evidence”. A genuinely self-refuting synthetic observation also triggered the guard. The heuristic scans observation text for phrases including `however` and `no evidence`; it does not compare the observation to the issue. These probes show a false-positive mechanism in the helper; they do not establish real report frequency or the truth of any absence claim. The interface can show `contested` in the fixqueue, and the rule changes severity rather than deleting the row.

Recommend: reserve definitive contradiction flags for deterministic, independently verified conflicts between a specific claim and the available script data; otherwise preserve the note's severity as provisional and use an internal “needs review” state, with the reason inspectable. Explicit absence claims still require sufficient coverage; don't upgrade them merely because they are unrefuted. Do not remove all checks: a false confident note can harm the writer. Do not call a model for every candidate by default: that adds latency and can introduce its own inaccuracies. Benchmark selective verification and review rates, false positives and false negatives across both scripts and wider multilingual examples. Keep the original label, evidence and reasons in the audit trail so changes are reversible; report uncertainty separately from impact/severity.

### Observation verification: semantic limits and option decision

Controlled helper tests using a fabricated two-scene document: `verify_observation` returned `True` for “Ajay never says hello to Venu” in a scene containing “Ajay tells Venu: hello my friend.” Its quoted phrase was separately `verified` at 1.0. Conversely, it returned `False` for “Venu is absent from this scene” when Venu's name did not appear in the cited scene. This helper checks certain names and quoted fragments, not negation, actual character presence or whether the observation's claim is logically supported. These are isolated helper results, not complete pipeline findings: other gates may detect or reject them, and missing a name alone does not prove a character is absent.

Recommend separate states: exact/approximate quote match (and location), mechanical checks performed and their results, claim support as unassessed unless actually adjudicated, and interpretive judgment as revisable. For incomplete parsing, missing coverage, negation or ambiguity, do not infer a confident contradiction simply from an absent name. Preserve current mechanical checks where defensible, with reasons and explicit unknown. Avoid a generic green 'verified' impression of the entire note. Consider small on-demand detail rather than badges on every sentence. Test later pipeline gates end-to-end before assigning actual release risk; measure verification precision/recall on real annotated cases, including absence and cross-scene references. A model-based semantic check is a possible extra signal, not proof, and should earn its latency through evaluation.

## Verification evaluation and performance decision record

**Decision:** retain a bounded grounding capability, but stop treating locating a citation as validation of the claim. Recommend a staged candidate (subject to results, not a commitment to a specific implementation): deterministic quote/scene checks and narrowly defined claim checks; abstain where scope, parsing or semantics are uncertain; offer further inspection where useful. Preserve interpretive notes as explicitly attributed readings rather than requiring everything to be mechanically decidable. Compare the current always-on bounded grounding pass, selective/priority-based grounding, and optional on-demand grounding under the same inputs. Do not assume a more expensive model or extra verifier is better.

**Current cost evidence:** `ObservationPass.run` loops sequentially over up to 24 `no_quote` findings by default; the source docstring describes ~40 seconds per target on a historical 26B setup with retries. This is repository-reported historical context, not a timing measurement in this session and not predictive for a different model/server. Its actual wall time, retry counts, token usage, concurrency effects and quality must be measured on target hardware. No performance claim is made from fake-client tests.

**Study protocol:**
1. Consent and local-only default; both supplied scripts remain private. Assemble a diversified, consented corpus including native-script and transliterated multilingual dialogue, shorts and long scripts, unusual narrative forms, parser ambiguity, deliberate open endings and direct emotional dialogue. Split projects by writer between development and held-out evaluation to avoid learning particular scenes; no external model or storage without informed consent.
2. Expert annotators independently judge (a) quote match and location, (b) narrow factual observation true/false/unknown given coverage, (c) whether evidence supports the interpretation, and (d) usefulness and potential harm to the writer. Record disagreement and adjudicate; subjective interpretation is not a single objective gold fact. Writer-stated intent is separate from independent reader/audience perception; no automatic veto and no assumption audience received it.
3. Include the two controlled contradictions, absence/cue parsing, unknown scenes, approximate quotation, non-ASCII, multiple valid readings, intentional ambiguity and post-gate enrichment. Check all relevant original pages, not just parser output. Score false confident claims and suppressed useful notes separately; measure recall as well as precision and abstentions, including abstained claims later judged checkable. Lexical-only `eval_recall.py` is not a semantic multilingual gate.
4. Compare complete candidate pipelines with the same approved model configurations, inputs, seed/settings where supported and multiple runs; record variation rather than exact wording matches. Report writer- and consultant-rated value and agency, parser coverage, claim/citation error rates, withheld/withdrawn notes, latency to first useful reading and final result, median/p95, tokens, retries, failures, memory and hardware. Stratify by script length/language and never conflate partial results with complete analysis.
5. Decisions require no regression on critical false-confidence/privacy cases, transparent incomplete coverage, and a jointly selected acceptable quality/latency trade-off on held-out scripts. Establish numerical targets from an observed baseline and writer research before locking them in. Keep readable uncertainty, optional review and an honest unavailable state when verification cannot run.

**Trade-offs:** a deterministic-only gate is fast but cannot decide rich meaning; model checks may improve some cases but can hallucinate and compete for compute; exhaustive verification delays the writer and may still miss errors; on-demand checks make the desk responsive but can leave more initial uncertainty. Selective grounding is the current recommendation to trial, not a demonstrated winner. Preserve analysis progress and do not quietly call an incomplete result complete.

### Grounding budget ordering: controlled test and decision

Directly exercised `ObservationPass.run` on a synthetic 26-finding list and a one-scene fabricated script with a recording fake client. All findings were `no_quote`; the first 25 were low severity and the last high. The default 24-call limit attempted notes 1–24 in input order and never attempted note 26. The fake returned no quotes; all 26 findings remain in the output, with the 24 attempted as ungrounded. This establishes selection order under the tested conditions, not that such a list appears frequently in real reports. The code's candidate set is specifically `no_quote`: failed quote/scene citations are separately excluded, so 'not attempted' is not synonymous with 'safe'.

**Decision:** test a transparent, bounded grounding queue ranked on evidence need, relevance, coverage, likely checkability and writer-selected interest, with fair diversity and stable tie-breaking. Expose whether a finding was attempted or not; allow a writer to request a specific follow-up. Preserve all findings and their original uncertainty while work proceeds. ## Storage, concurrency and backup review — additional closure work

- `screenplay_studio/jsonio.py` uses per-path thread and OS locks, unique temporary files, fsync and atomic replacement, with best-effort directory fsync. This makes a single store write robust; it is not an atomic transaction spanning several files. `revision.py` itself documents that working text, edit history and redo files can diverge if a crash occurs between their individual writes. Generation publication must account for both the report pair and the edited-draft/log boundary; it must not claim global transactionality merely because each JSON write is atomic.
- The webapp's `_ANALYZE_LOCKS` are process-local `threading.Lock`s; CLI/watch call `Orchestrator.run_analyze` directly. In a controlled temporary project, two independently loaded manifests and two threads both entered the real orchestrator's fake `analyze` call simultaneously and returned without error. This establishes that direct orchestrators do not serialize one another in the tested setup; it does not by itself prove cross-process corruption or a production incident. Cross-process serialization or generation-safe compare-and-swap at publication must cover **all** entry points and be tested with separate processes, not just webapp tabs. Avoid holding the writer's working-draft lock across inference.
- Backup currently walks the project and archives files, skipping sidecars and temporary files; per-file OSError is silently skipped. It is not a coherent multi-file snapshot, so a concurrent backup may mix generations or omit files without that omission being reflected in the per-project backup. This is source analysis, not a tested real backup failure. A consistent generation manifest plus explicit missing-file accounting is preferable; don't claim a backup is complete without verifying it. Backup exclusion of `writer_profile.json` from the library archive is intentional in the current implementation and should be disclosed when describing recovery scope.
- `ProjectManifest` can store an API key. A Flask test client against a temporary, fabricated project containing a **synthetic non-secret marker** confirmed the per-project backup includes `project.json` and retains that marker in `api_key`. This is a credential-exposure risk if a user has configured a real key and shares the archive; no real credential was read or used. Library backup traverses project directories similarly, but that endpoint was not independently exercised. Recommend redacted project manifests in shareable/exportable backups by default, explicit disclosure and separately controlled credential restoration; never silently include a real token. Test nested files, existing archives, permissions and restore without requiring credentials in chat.

**Decision and caveat:** recommend immutable generations with a cross-process publication guard and a single atomic published pointer; establish generation durability and an explicit crash-recovery protocol before claiming a transaction. Evaluate a journal or equivalent for draft + edit history, versus storing immutable draft revisions with history derived from the published revision. Both need migration, disk-space and performance review; a single pointer alone cannot retroactively make existing multi-file editing transactional. For backups, prefer a manifest of included versions and skipped files, redacted secrets, and a restore-validation test; make any unavoidable concurrent inconsistencies explicit. Keep projects local and preserve writer control. These are proposed designs, not implemented fixes.
