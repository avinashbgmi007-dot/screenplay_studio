design notes — 16 attributes · v2
1 · PremiseThe studio is a warm one-screen screening house at 2am: your script is the picture — projected as readable paper — the doctor's notes are the director's cues synced beside it, and scenes are chapters on the strip below. 2 · Reader & momentThe writer who wants the cinema identity without paying for it in writing comfort: vertical reading on real paper, the horizontal strip demoted to a jump navigator. 3 · Emotional registerHouse lights low, amber chapter lights on. The page glows like the screen itself; the booth is warm dark, never blue-black. 4 · Spatial metaphorThe paper page IS the projected frame and the room's light source; director's notes ride a 320px rail; the chapter strip sits below (act reels keep 50+ scenes legible); the green room holds the people. 5 · Type systemCourier Prime 16px on paper for the script (13:1); Caveat for Sameer's cues and tape stickies; IBM Plex Mono 10px+ for keys and labels — v1's 9.5px whispers retired; Source Serif 4 for prose. All shipped fonts, no additions. 6 · Chromatic systemWarm booth (#0d0b08 family — Night Room donor, zero blue-blacks) + amber keys + moon for Sushruta + teal for machine status. Paper #f4e9d3 with 13.04:1 ink. 25/25 audited pairs pass; severity never colors words on paper — dot + wash only. 7 · Light logicThe paper is the projector: the page glow lights the booth from center; amber lives on chapter marks and scene keys; everything else recedes into warm dark. 8 · Navigation modelReading is the only required axis — vertical, on paper. The strip is a chapter navigator: click a chapter, land on its page. Every deck tool is a visible labeled word at 10px+; no keyboard faith required (j/k remain as shortcuts, not prerequisites). 9 · Feedback representationFindings as wash + dot on the exact paper line, words never recolored; director's notes keyed scene-first ("sc. 3 · the watch · 14:28" — timecode demoted to flavor); locate is bidirectional and wired: click a line, its note lights and scrolls; click a note, its line pulses on the page. 10 · Partner presenceSameer's cue sheet rides the notes rail in screening; the lobby sidecard; full seats in the green room. One presence per surface, one voice each — never doubled. 11 · Chat compositionCue sheets and seat lines — handwritten lines with a single ask each, not bubble stacks; the green-room composer addresses the room, and carries any page selection along. 12 · Motion philosophyThe reel turns while threading; chapter progress animates on the active card; projector flicker for thinking ("thinking it through" text preserved); locate pulses twice, then stops. Reduced-motion honored globally. 13 · Composer disciplineAll inputs on warm booth dark with light text (11.7:1+); focus rings brighten amber — backgrounds never flip (hate-list #5). 14 · Migration costMedium — vertical reading maps directly onto the shipped manuscript DOM; the strip is additive chrome, not a rewrite; wash/dot finding lines reuse Phase-13 surface patterns; no new fonts, no new geometry. 15 · Risk exposureThe strip must stay a navigator and never grow into a reading surface; paper-on-dark needs shadow discipline (one soft shadow + one contact shadow, consistently); the rail must not creep past 320px or it becomes a pane (hate-list #2). 16 · One-line verdictV1's cinema identity with the Night Room's body — the screening house you can actually write in.
