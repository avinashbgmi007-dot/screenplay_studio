## 2. What the field actually does (this is not decoration)

I traced every consumer. `confidence_tier` reaches the model through **two**
channels, and they disagree slightly:

**Channel 1 — a per-rule gloss, three distinct messages** (`knowledge_base/knowledge_base.py:73-74`,
inside `to_prompt_fragment()`, so it ships with every rule, in every prompt):

| tier | what the model is told |
|---|---|
| `high` | *"treat findings as **near-certain** if detection_signal is met"* |
| `medium` | *"treat findings as a **judgment call**"* |
| `low` | *"frame findings as a **discussion prompt, not a verdict**"* |

**Channel 2 — a global instruction** (`screenplay_analyzer/prompts.py:49`, injected into
**all five** analyzer prompts — lines 131, 162, 176, 193, 267):

> "For **'high' or 'medium'** tier principles, state findings directly. For 'low' tier
> principles, phrase the finding as a genuine open question…"

So: **`high` and `medium` are tonally identical in the global instruction.** The only
place they differ is the per-rule gloss — "near-certain" vs "judgment call". And
`low` is the only tier that changes the *shape* of the finding (question vs assertion).

**Channel 3 — budget ordering** (`screenplay_analyzer/rules_context.py:191`): when
`SCREENPLAY_KB_BUDGET` is set, rules are sorted by tier and `high` is kept first.
Default is `0` (unlimited), so this is latent today. See §6.4 — it points the wrong way.

**Blast radius if applied:** prompt text only. No test asserts a tier value or count
(`tests/test_kb_schema.py:68` only checks the tier is one of the three allowed values;
`:117` recomputes `stats()` dynamically). Nothing routes on the tier. So this is a
**tone change**, not a behavioural one — and that is exactly its limit (§7).

## 3. What the field *should* mean — the rubric I applied

The schema defines the tiers, so I used its own words as the test
(`knowledge_base/README.md:77-89`), and judged each rule by its **`detection_signal`**
— which the schema calls "the operational definition, not just the theory".

- **T1 → `high`.** The answer is **bounded once the data exists**: a placement against a
  range, a presence/absence of a named element, a count, a direct contradiction between
  two explicit statements, or a lexical pattern. Two careful readers agree without
  debating taste. *(The README's own example: "does this beat exist within its expected
  page range once we know page count".)*
- **T2 → `medium`.** An **observable pattern** you can point at in the text, but deciding
  whether it is present — or "enough" — needs interpretation.
- **T3 → `low`.** A verdict about **quality even among experts** — "earned",
  "satisfying", "effective", "formidable". Honest framing is a question, not a claim.

The rubric is stated per rule in the CSV (`criterion` column), so any call you disagree
with is arguable in the open rather than hidden.

## 4. Your question, answered

> *"I think genre/theme shall be high, rest can be medium low… I think genre can also go
> to medium state? while theme stays?"*

**First, a correction that matters: that is essentially the current state.** Every genre
file is already 100% `high`, and `theme.json` is already 100% `high`. So "genre/theme
high, rest medium/low" would change very little — the problem is not *which files* are
high, it is that **202 of 263 rules are**, including most of the "rest".

On the two specific calls:

### Theme should **not** stay high — the KB already disagrees with itself here

- The README's own canonical example of a `low` rule is *"does this controlling idea feel
  earned"* — a theme question.
- The KB **already tags the two generic theme rules `low`**: `controlling_idea` and
  `theme_contradiction` (both in `story_macro.json`).
- The two rules in `theme.json` are the same class of claim:
  - `martell_theme_through_characters` — "is theme dramatised through choices or stated in
    dialogue?" That is an **observable structural pattern** → **medium**.
  - `weiland_theme_from_arc` — its detection_signal is literally *"stories where the theme
    feels preachy or disconnected"* → **low**.

Keeping theme at `high` would mean the model is told to assert, as near-certain, a claim
the KB's own generic rule frames as a discussion prompt. **Recommendation: medium + low.**

### Genre should be **mostly medium, with a real low minority** — not uniformly either

The genre files are **convention checklists**: "is there a ticking clock", "does the
villain's plan land before the midpoint", "is the reveal before the climax". Those are
observable patterns → **medium**. A subset are outright quality verdicts → **low**:
`action_villain_strength` ("formidable"), `mystery_solution_satisfying` ("satisfying"),
`comedy_character_flaws_funny` ("funny"), `drama_pacing_meditative` ("adequate time"),
`romance_grand_gesture` ("earned, not generic").

**The single sharpest piece of evidence — and it is not a matter of taste:**

> `genre_convention_fulfillment` in `story_macro.json` — the **generic** rule that says
> *"check the genre's standard beats are present, flag conspicuous absences"* — is tagged
> **`low`**.
>
> Its **90 genre-specific instances** (the action/comedy/horror/mystery/romance/scifi/
> thriller/drama files) are tagged **`high`** — 89 of 90.

The same idea cannot be a discussion prompt in the abstract and a near-certain verdict in
every concrete case. That inconsistency is the strongest sign the genre tagging was never
a considered decision — it was a default.

**Genre-tagged subset (90 rules):** today `89 high / 1 medium` → proposed
**`65 medium / 21 low / 4 high`**.

### What I recommend in one line

**Neither genre nor theme as a category — per rule, by the rubric.** Category-level
tagging is what produced the problem; re-tagging by category would only move it.

## 6. Findings beyond the tiers (found while doing this)

### 6.1 Stored mojibake in 21 of 26 KB files — it reaches the model verbatim

**135 mangled em-dashes** are stored as JSON `\u` escapes — the file literally contains
`\u00e2\u20ac\u201d` where `—` belongs. Because `to_prompt_fragment()` includes
`definition` and `detection_signal` verbatim, the model receives:

> `…from the story's beginning to its end â€" not just a change in circumstances.`

Worst files: `scene.json` (18), `character.json` (17), `plot_thread.json` (14),
`structure_pacing.json` (11). Meanwhile `continuity.json`, `relationship.json` and
`story_macro.json` store a **proper** `—` (13 occurrences) — so the KB is internally
inconsistent, which is the signature of a wrong codec at save time.

**FIXED — this is now repaired** (it needed no product decision, so it shipped while the
tier question stayed open). All 135 occurrences repaired across 24 files, plus two rule
**names** (`bell_fictive_dream`, `zinsser_dump_the_clutter` — the debris was visible in
report titles) and one definition that also carried two mangled Chinese words
(`bell_midpoint_shift`; the foreign word is dropped, its English gloss kept). Every KB rule
file is now **pure ASCII**, with legitimate punctuation stored as `\u` escapes — so a later
wrong-codec save cannot reintroduce this class of bug at all. Guarded by
`tests/test_kb_text_hygiene.py` (4 tests; all 4 fail on the pre-fix KB).

*Method note: my first two checks disagreed and both were wrong — the byte scan looked for
the mojibake's UTF-8 bytes, but the mangling is stored as **ASCII escapes**, so it was
invisible to that scan. Settled by dumping the decoded string and the raw bytes side by
side. I nearly reported the opposite conclusion.*

### 6.3 `medium` becomes broad — that is honest, but say it out loud

198 of 263 rules land on `medium` (75%). A reviewer could fairly ask whether `medium` has
become a dumping ground. It has not: the schema defines it as *"needs real interpretive
judgment, but the judgment call is narrow and well-specified"*, and most craft rules are
exactly that. But because the **global** instruction already treats `high` and `medium`
identically, the visible change for those 158 rules is **only the per-rule gloss**
("near-certain" → "judgment call"). That is the change we want, but it is a smaller
visible effect than the 158-rule count suggests.

### 6.4 A design tension worth deciding deliberately

`rules_context.py:191` keeps **`high` first** under budget pressure. But if `high` means
"near-certain", that ordering preserves the rules that need the **least** help and drops
the ones where the model is most likely to go wrong without a definition. Two coherent
options: keep it (ground the mechanical checks first) or **invert** it (ground the
interpretive rules first). Latent today because the default budget is unlimited — but it
should be a decision, not an accident.

tagging problem are the same drift.

### 6.6 The KB mixes novel-craft into a screenplay product

21 rules use prose-fiction vocabulary — `revision.json` ("prose", "chapter"),

"The reader" is normal screenwriting parlance and not a problem; "chapter" and "manuscript"

## 7. What this does NOT do — the honest limits

1. **It is a prompt instruction, not a guarantee.** The model may not obey the gloss. I
   cannot verify the tone change without a live model, and I did not. Anyone claiming this
   measurably improves output would be guessing.
2. **It does not change which rules reach a prompt.** Routing is by `taxonomy_level`
   only. Changing *what the model sees* is a different, unmeasurable-without-a-model
   change, and I deliberately left it out.
3. **197 rules moving at once is a big single step.** The report's voice changes across the
   board. If you'd rather stage it, the natural first tranche is the 33 `high → low` moves
   (the outright taste verdicts) — highest signal, lowest risk.
4. **I did not touch `severity_default`.** It is a separate field (149 medium / 71 high /
   43 low) with its own meaning, and conflating the two would be a different change.
5. **A more conservative target is available.** If 5% `high` feels too thin, the defensible
   place to add highs is the placement/presence family — the ~8 sequence/reel checks
   (`gulino_sequence_climax`, `comedy_reel_laugh`, `horror_reel_event_requirement`, …)
   whose *partition* is mechanical even though the content test is a judgment. That would
   land `high` nearer 8–10% and is a reasonable alternative reading of the schema.

## 8. Critique of this proposal

- **I was wrong twice and corrected both before reporting.** First I concluded the KB was
  clean on a byte scan that structurally could not see escape-encoded mojibake. Then I
  nearly filed the 8 dangling `related_rules` as a bug — they are a **deliberately
  documented to-author list** with a subset guard (`tests/test_kb_schema.py:27-91`). Both
  caught by checking the claim before writing it down.
- **The rubric is mine, not the KB's.** The schema defines the tiers in prose; turning that
  into a testable three-way rule is an interpretation. I have stated it per rule so you can
  overrule individual calls, but you should know the framework itself is a judgment.
- **`medium` is doing most of the work.** 75% of the KB. If you disagree with my T2/T3
  boundary, the proposal shifts a lot — that boundary is the load-bearing decision, more
  than any individual call.
- **14 `high` is a strong claim.** It says "only these 14 things can be asserted as
  facts". I believe that is right by the schema's own definition, but it is a real
  position, not a neutral tidy-up. §7.5 offers the more conservative reading.
- **Nothing was verified against a live model** — the one thing that would settle whether
  the tone actually changes. Stated in §7.1 rather than buried.
