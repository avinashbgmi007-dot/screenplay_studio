design notes — 16 attributes
1 · PremiseThe studio is a screening room: your script is the picture, the doctor's notes are the director's cues, scenes are frames on a strip. Reading your own work becomes watching it. 2 · Reader & momentThe writer who thinks in scenes and cuts — who wants the manuscript staged, not scrolled, and diagnosis keyed to timecode. 3 · Emotional registerHouse lights down, projector up. Amber timecodes on booth dark; the hush of a reel running. 4 · Spatial metaphorA projection booth: manuscript staged center as the slate, director's notes synced right, filmstrip spine below (act-clustered reels so 50+ scenes survive), green room for the people. 5 · Type systemCourier Prime for the slate; Caveat for Sameer's cues and tape stickies; IBM Plex Mono for timecodes; Source Serif 4 for posters. All shipped fonts, no additions. 6 · Chromatic systemBooth dark (#08090c) + timecode amber (#e0b45c) + mark-red (high) + colorist teal (Sushruta's voice). 14/14 contrast pairs pass AA — the most robust palette of the five. 7 · Light logicThe projector beam: a faint warm wash from above the slate; amber glows only on the active frame. Everything else is booth dark. 8 · Navigation modelThe filmstrip IS the nav — scenes as frames, j/k to step, act reels to collapse; the deck rail holds draft tools; the mockbar is state-only. 9 · Feedback representationDirector's notes synced to the frame with timecodes + severity marks on the strip cards; tape stickies for the writer's own notes, taped INTO the slate (hate-list #1/#7). 10 · Partner presenceThe green room: Sameer and Sushruta as seats with cues and actions; Sameer also cues in the lobby and on the slate's cuesheet — same person, stage-appropriate surfaces, one voice each (hate-list #10). 11 · Chat compositionCue sheets and seat lines — handwritten cues with a single ask line each, not bubble stacks; the green-room composer addresses the room. 12 · Motion philosophyThe reel turns while threading; stripAdvance on the active frame; projFlicker for thinking ("thinking it through" preserved). Reduced-motion honored. 13 · Composer disciplineAll inputs on booth dark with light text (gr-ask 15:1, cuesheet 14:1, askline 14:1); focus brightens the border amber, never flips backgrounds (hate-list #5). 14 · Migration costHigh — horizontal geography fights the vertical-scroll writing habit; the strip needs its own virtualization. The slate itself maps cleanly, though. 15 · Risk exposurePer-scene staging is charming at 7 scenes but must prove itself at 50; act reels mitigate, but j/k keyboard faith is still required; the deck rail must stay sparse. 16 · One-line verdictThe most cinematic and the most fun — a director's view of your own script, if you accept the projection booth's geometry.
